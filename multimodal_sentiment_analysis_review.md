# Systematic Literature Review: Multimodal Sentiment Analysis and Emotion Recognition

This document provides a systematic literature review on Multimodal Sentiment Analysis and Emotion Recognition, focusing on the fusion mechanisms of Text, Audio, and Vision data from recent research (2020–2026).

| **Gated Cross-Modal Attention Network (GCMA-Net)**, 2023 (Various Authors) | Gated Cross-Modal Attention | Dynamically adjusts modality contributions at the sample level, effectively suppressing noisy or irrelevant signals during fusion. | Tuning the gating mechanism can be difficult, and it can be highly sensitive to domain shifts in testing data. | CH-SIMS |
| **Enhance-then-Balance Modality Collaboration (EBMC)**, 2023 (Various Authors) | Enhance-then-Balance (Hybrid Cross-attention) | Prevents dominant modalities (like text) from overshadowing weaker ones (audio/vision), ensuring truly multimodal robust fusion. | Added architectural complexity in balancing dynamic weights across batches during the training phase. | CMU-MOSI, CMU-MOSEI |

## State-of-the-Art Trends in Multimodal Fusion

The current trajectory of multimodal sentiment analysis and emotion recognition demonstrates a clear paradigm shift away from simple early (feature-level) and late (decision-level) fusion methods towards sophisticated, Transformer-based cross-attention architectures. Traditional early and late fusion techniques often struggle with the "curse of dimensionality" and fail to capture the complex, asynchronous interactions between text, audio, and visual data. In contrast, cross-attention mechanisms allow models to dynamically weigh the importance of one modality based on the context provided by another. By computing query, key, and value vectors across different data streams, these networks can effectively align emotionally salient features—such as matching a subtle facial expression or vocal prosody with specific spoken words—while simultaneously suppressing irrelevant noise.

Furthermore, recent advancements have focused on addressing the limitations of early Transformer models by introducing hybrid and modality-balanced architectures. State-of-the-art systems now frequently incorporate gating mechanisms, recursive attention, and graph neural networks to handle real-world challenges such as missing modalities, noisy signals, and modality imbalance (where a dominant signal like text overpowers audio or vision). Text-oriented approaches that use textual semantics as an anchor to guide the processing of non-verbal cues have proven particularly effective. Overall, the field is prioritizing architectures that not only fuse heterogeneous data deeply and adaptively but also maintain robustness in imperfect, "in-the-wild" scenarios, despite the trade-offs of increased computational complexity and memory overhead.
